# Johannesburg civic systems improvement portfolio

**Research snapshot:** 14 October 2026  
**Status:** Proposed, evidence-led portfolio. It is not a municipal mandate, an assertion of access to City systems, or a claim that any proposed integration has been deployed.

## Mission

Improve the everyday reliability, affordability, safety, dignity, and opportunity of life in Johannesburg by finding and fixing recurring failures across connected services. Start with public information and voluntary community/partner data; earn access to operational data through explicit agreements; measure outcomes independently.

The goal is not to create one more disconnected civic app. It is to make existing services easier to navigate, recurring failures easier to detect, repair responsibilities clearer, and public claims easier to verify.

## What official evidence already tells us

- On 27 May 2026, the City presented a R97.1 billion 2026/27 medium-term budget. The City's published summary reported R8.8 billion in capital budget for 2026/27, a R25.3 billion medium-term capital plan, 60% of capital investment directed to historically marginalised areas, and R7.2 billion for public safety. It also reported a revised revenue collection rate of 86%, down from 88.6%. These are City-reported budget figures and targets, not independently verified outcomes. A civic ledger should track what was allocated, contracted, delivered, and independently verified separately.
- Johannesburg Water's 23 July 2026 announcement described smart hydraulic flow controls at 28 high-consumption reservoirs/towers, 306 smart pressure controllers being installed, and 225 acoustic noise loggers installed with a further 125 planned by October. Those planned completion dates have passed; actual commissioning and effectiveness need confirmation rather than inference.
- The City announced a multi-stakeholder illegal-dumping and cleanup operation in Region E in August 2026 and said Pikitup had resumed operations in most areas while some challenges remained. This makes repeat-hotspot tracking and collection reliability more useful than one-off cleanup counts.
- The City's transport department and Johannesburg Development Agency described the Comprehensive Integrated Transport Plan moving into a 12-month phase for transport indicators and target setting. A civic portfolio should support that work with measurable accessibility and reliability indicators, not invent transport targets on the City's behalf.
- Johannesburg Roads Agency's Operation Restore describes coordinated pothole, pavement, stormwater, and traffic-signal maintenance. A service ledger can expose recurrence and downstream dependencies, not just count work orders closed.
- The City has described an urban agriculture/food-resilience programme and regional agri-resource centres, and reported launch of an eHealth system at Claremont Clinic in October 2026. These provide existing programmes to connect with, rather than duplicate.

Sources are listed at the end. Claims above intentionally distinguish an announcement or target from verified delivery.

They are not a claim that one neighbourhood is more deserving than another. Select pilot locations only after a transparent review of incident burden, service deprivation, data coverage, community consent, and delivery capacity.

## The highest-leverage design: a common service case and evidence model

Use a common record that can represent a pothole, water interruption, blocked drain, missed collection, broken light, unsafe crossing, or housing-project milestone without flattening their domain-specific facts.

Minimum fields:

- Stable case ID plus the original official reference, if one exists.
- Service domain, issue category, affected area at an appropriate privacy level, received time, and last-updated time.
- Status transitions as append-only events, with actor/source and timestamp; corrections append a correction event instead of erasing history.
- Evidence pointers and digests, capture time, source, consent/licence, quality flags and verification state.
- Responsible entity and escalation path only where evidenced; unknown ownership stays unknown.
- Severity, safety impact, repeat history and affected-service context, with the rationale exposed.
- Resolution claim, supporting evidence, and whether a resident or independent reviewer confirmed it.
- Retention, disclosure, privacy classification, and opt-out/redaction policy.

Treat a report as an allegation or signal until corroborated. A case being marked “closed” by an internal system is not the same as the underlying problem being fixed. A missing report is not proof the problem does not exist; report volumes are biased by connectivity, trust, language, and awareness.

## Transparent prioritisation without pretending to know more than we do

A first triage model should be a published rubric, not an opaque AI score. Consider:

1. **Immediate harm:** danger to life, health, access to emergency services, or essential services.
2. **Extent and duration:** number of people/services affected, outage duration, and repeat incidents.
3. **Equity:** service deprivation and barriers to reporting; do not use raw complaint volume as a proxy for need.
4. **Dependencies:** whether a failure is blocking several other services, such as power to pumping or a blocked drain flooding a road.
5. **Evidence confidence and freshness:** official confirmation, corroboration, recency, and whether a critical fact remains unknown.
6. **Remediability:** safe actions available, accountable owner, materials/workforce, legal constraints, and potential unintended effects.

Safety-critical hazards should have an explicit escalation override. Otherwise, publish the factor-level explanation, allow human review and corrections, and measure whether ranking errors disproportionately disadvantage low-connectivity or historically underserved areas. Never let a model automatically dispatch scarce public services or decide eligibility for housing or social assistance.

## A first 90-day sequence

### Days 0–15: establish a reliable baseline
- Use the City's 2026/27 IDP, SDBIP, service standards charter, entity business plans, and budget as the official target register.
- Create a source inventory with URL, publication date, owner, licence/access restrictions, last checked, and whether the source is a commitment, a progress statement, a raw record, or an independently verified outcome.
- Pick one pilot boundary using documented criteria, not intuition: one manageable neighbourhood/precinct with repeat issues across at least two services, a willing community contact, available reporting channels, and a feasible accountable partner.
- Agree data protection and consent rules before collecting residents' photos, names, numbers or home locations.

### Days 16–30: build the common civic ledger
- Implement append-only case events, source/evidence provenance, duplicate candidates, owner/status uncertainty, privacy tiers, and exported CSV/JSON.
- Start with public and opt-in records; do not make unapproved operational API calls or scrape personal data.
- Preserve municipal reference numbers so the tool helps residents follow official processes rather than splintering them.

### Days 31–60: run two narrow service workflows
- **Workflow A:** potholes/blocked drains/broken lights, because site inspection and before/after evidence can verify many cases.
- **Workflow B:** water/electricity interruptions, first from published notices and resident-confirmed timing; add utility telemetry only by agreement.
- For dumping, pilot a repeat-hotspot lifecycle and correct routing to Pikitup, City Parks or environmental health depending on ownership and land type.

### Days 61–90: independently evaluate
- Freeze the baseline period and evaluation definitions before comparing.
- Report medians and distributions of acknowledgement, repair and verification times; repeated faults; reopened cases; coverage by ward/service/access channel; and missing-data rates.
- Report both successes and unknowns. If the pilot cannot demonstrate real improvement, narrow or redesign it instead of declaring success from activity counts.

The 90-day plan is a proposed schedule contingent on access, staffing and community participation; it is not a promise that municipal response times or physical infrastructure can be changed by software alone.

## Implementation boundaries for Luminous Dynamics

- **Sol-Atlas:** spatial context, public asset layers, issue maps, dependency views, timelines and explicitly labelled observed/derived/scenario overlays.
- **Mycelix:** accountable evidence receipts, attribution, consent/stewardship rules, governance history and correction/revocation semantics. Do not place sensitive personal or high-resolution critical-infrastructure details in a public DHT by default.
- **Symtropy:** deterministic replay, scenario comparison, dependency analysis and failure injection; validate against independent records.
- **Symthaea:** read-only anomaly hypotheses, clusters, risk explanations, uncertainty and follow-up recommendations; never treat a hypothesis as a verified fault.
- **Municipal/NGO/community partners:** retain authority over their systems, service priority, field dispatch and adjudication.

Start with open, documented data contracts and portable exports. Do not create a brittle integration that only one team can operate. No autonomous connection to pumps, breakers, traffic signals, access-control systems or other operational technology is included.

## What is implemented now versus still proposed

The public Sol-Atlas branch contains a reusable Rust contract at `sol-atlas-core/src/civic_cases.rs` and exports it from the core crate. It currently models service domains, severity, privacy classification, official references, evidence review state, append-only status events, and a derived current status.

The contract checks:
- required case/category/area identifiers and schema version;
- unique official references, evidence IDs, and event IDs;
- SHA-256 digest syntax and HTTPS-only public reference links without query/fragment tokens;
- monotonic record time and consistency between the last event and the case's updated time;
- evidence references must resolve to known evidence IDs;
- legal status transitions, evidence-linked resolution claims, and independent review evidence before the `ResolutionVerified` event is accepted;
- correction events carry a reason code and evidence but do not rewrite current status or prior history;
- a public projection is returned only for explicitly `PublicCase` records, requires a separate region/ward/precinct `PublicAreaReference`, and never reuses the internal area reference. It filters private evidence and suppresses actor references, raw official ticket IDs, and artifact pointers. The prefix/granularity check is a guardrail, not proof that the underlying geography is correctly classified.

The module has unit tests authored for normal cases, duplicate/out-of-order events, resolution evidence, independent verification, reopening, append-only corrections, public projection redaction, and unsafe public URLs. The latest head's `fmt`, `test-native`, and `check-wasm` jobs are queued; these tests must not be described as passing until the exact head completes successfully.

This is not yet an application or a production case-management service. Persistence, access control, authentication, evidence-byte hashing, retention/deletion policy, redaction review, import/export adapters, map UI, resident intake, official ticket-system integration, and human dispatch workflows remain unimplemented. The type's `PublicCase` visibility is an explicit publication gate, not a substitute for area-level privacy review or legal authority to publish.

## Evaluation scorecard

Every pilot should publish a baseline and then track:
- Resident impact: service outage hours, hazards removed, successful referrals, paid work and housing outcomes where the project can legitimately measure them.
- Resolution: median and 90th-percentile time to acknowledgement, assignment, attendance, repair and independent verification.
- Recurrence: repeat failures within 30/90 days and reopened cases.
- Equity: outcomes and data coverage by ward/service area and reporting channel, with privacy protection; compare need-adjusted outcomes rather than raw counts alone.
- Public value: cost per verified resolution, maintenance avoided where causally supported, verified project delivery versus budget, and payment/job outcomes.
- Trust and evidence: source traceability, correction latency, stale/unknown data, duplicate handling, privacy incidents, and proportion of closed cases independently checked.
- Safety: no unauthorised actuation, no public leakage of sensitive topology or personal case information, and a logged human decision for consequential priorities.

Do not set numeric performance targets until the baseline and responsible service owner's published standard are confirmed.
