# South African above-ground build compliance and approvals crosswalk v0.1

**As of:** 7 November 2026  
**Status:** research register; not legal advice, not a statutory approval and not an interpretation for a specific project.

## Why this is now a first-class CityMaker workstream

A product or construction technique cannot be called buildable solely because it can be designed, simulated, manufactured or assembled. The project must have an applicable, current and version-matched route through building control, product/system conformity, professional responsibility, construction safety, environmental/site approvals, procurement (where relevant), inspection and occupation.

The rule is **evidence for the exact scope**, not a generic “approved technology” badge.

## Critical current status findings

### SANS 10400-XA:2026, Edition 3 — edition-status correction (7 November 2026)

The earlier 2021 Edition 2 references are no longer a safe default for the latest edition. SAIAT's professional CPD portal identifies an event on **SANS 10400-XA 3rd Edition 2026**, states that Edition 3 was published early in 2026, and identifies the presenter as chair of SABS TC 060 and a member of the latest review workgroup. The course description specifically flags changes to the method for satisfying energy-efficient hot-water requirements. [SAIAT CPD source](https://cpd.saiat.org.za/sans-10400-xa-3rd-edition-cc)

This is credible specialist secondary evidence of a published 2026 Edition 3, but it is not a substitute for the copyrighted SABS publication or a project-specific legal/applicability determination. The exact SABS catalogue record, publication/approval metadata, effective date, transition treatment and full text have **not** been independently retrieved for this register.

Required treatment:

- Record **SANS 10400-XA:2026, Edition 3** as the latest identified edition candidate; do not describe the 2021 Edition 2 as the latest edition.
- Keep the compliance state **Version_or_Applicability_Unknown** until the exact SABS catalogue record and lawful copy are checked and the applicable building use/project date are known.
- Acquire and review the official SABS edition under appropriate access rights before encoding clause-level checks. Do not reconstruct copyrighted requirements from course notes or secondary summaries.
- Compare Edition 3 with Edition 2, paying particular attention to the hot-water compliance route identified by SAIAT; verify every other change from the official text rather than assuming there are none.
- Bind each compliance decision to the exact edition, effective/transition dates, building class, calculation method, qualified responsible person, project/design revision and relevant building-control decision.
- Treat older municipal or international policy summaries that still say 2021 as historical evidence until their edition references are refreshed.

This correction does not claim that the standard itself amends the National Building Regulations, that every project must use the same compliance route, or that any Highveld Keep design is compliant.

### Public procurement: important September 2026 correction

The Constitutional Court's judgment in *Premier of the Western Cape Government and Another v Speaker of the National Assembly and Others; City of Cape Town and Others v Speaker of the National Assembly and Others* [2026] ZACC 37, delivered 17 September 2026, declared the Public Procurement Act 28 of 2024 invalid because the public-participation process was constitutionally inadequate. The declaration was not suspended; the judgment notes the Act had not been brought into operation and that existing legislation continued to govern. Therefore CityMaker must **not** use the 2024 Act as if it were the operative public-procurement statute. Each project must bind its governing framework as at the procurement date. [Judgment](https://www3.saflii.org/za/cases/ZACC/2026/37.html) [Finance Minister statement](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/minister-enoch-godongwana-notes-and-respects-judgement-constitutional-court-i)

A further draft second edition of the cidb Standard for Uniformity in Construction Procurement was published in Gazette 55409 (Board Notice 986) on 18 September 2026, after the July 2026 draft. It remains a **draft**, not a final operative standard. The cidb’s prescripts page continues to distinguish current prescripts/guidance from the draft. Do not build a procurement workflow against the draft. Retrieve the exact Gazette 55409 text and confirm the consultation process and any later final notice before making a project claim. [Government Gazette listing](https://www.gov.za/document?field_gcisdoc_doctype=All&field_gcisdoc_subjects=All&search_query=procurement) [cidb prescripts](https://www.cidb.org.za/clients/procurement-prescripts/cidb-prescripts/)

### Agrément: target date is not publication evidence

Agrément South Africa published a notice saying it was reviewing its building, walling and product-system performance criteria, including structural strength/stability/serviceability, fire, water/damp, acoustics, thermal/energy/condensation, durability and technical-drawing guidance, with a target completion date of 31 October 2026. That page is a review notice; it does not itself prove that the revised criteria were released. Treat the criteria version as **Unknown pending confirmation from Agrément**, not as automatically updated on the target date. [Certification process](https://agrement.co.za/certification-process/) [Criteria review notice](https://agrement.co.za/important-notification-review-of-agrement-south-africa-performance-criteria/)

### NHBRC IBT database: useful discovery, not blanket project approval

NHBRC publishes dynamic IBT database spreadsheets and describes continuing structural-performance research on registered systems because performance information is limited. Capture a dated database snapshot and verify the exact system, holder, scope and project applicability. A listing does not automatically approve every design, supplier, site, use or altered product revision. [NHBRC IBT page](https://www.nhbrc.org.za/innovative-building-technologies/)

### Construction safety and competency regulations

The 3 July 2026 launch speech for the National Built Environment and Construction Safety Framework announced that CBE Public Interest and Safety Regulations would be gazetted, addressing structural/dolomitic occurrences, erection compliance and certification aligned to SANS 17024. The announcement is not a Gazette or proof of commencement. The operative regulation status therefore remains **Announced_Not_Verified_Gazetted** until the actual Gazette and effective date are obtained. [Government announcement](https://www.gov.za/news/speeches/minister-dean-macpherson-launch-national-built-environment-and-construction-safety)

The Department of Employment and Labour published draft Construction Regulations 2025 for comment, intended to replace Construction Regulations 2014. The operative status of any final replacement must be checked from official sources before project execution; draft language must not be treated as current law. [Department notice](https://www.labour.gov.za/Media-Desk/Media-Statements/Pages/Department-of-Employment-and-Labour-invites-public-comments-on-the-repeal-of-Construction-Regulations.aspx)

## Compliance layers to map per project

1. National Building Regulations and Building Standards Act 103 of 1977, current National Building Regulations, relevant SANS 10400 parts and municipal building control.
2. Project/building-use-specific energy, fire, structural, moisture, acoustic, thermal, accessibility, durability and health requirements.
3. Agrément certificate route for non-standardised products/systems where applicable, including exact version, certificate scope, holder, conditions, expiry and changes.
4. NHBRC registration/enrolment, manual, IBT status and inspections for covered homebuilding activity.
5. Site/geotechnical and dolomitic risk, professional design and independent review where required.
6. Occupational health and safety law, operative construction regulations, appointments, risk assessments and construction work permit/notification requirements where applicable.
7. CBE/professional council registration, competency and certification provisions, based on operative instruments rather than announcements.
8. City of Johannesburg zoning/land-use, building plan, inspections, occupation certification and separate public-realm/road-reserve approvals.
9. Environmental, water-use, waste, heritage and ecological permissions according to the activity/site.
10. cidb contractor registration/grading and the correct public infrastructure procurement prescripts for public works.
11. Product/equipment-specific SANS/ISO/IEC standards and statutory regimes (electrical, lifts, pressure systems, lifting plant and equipment safety).

The list is a screening taxonomy, not a claim that all provisions apply to every building or asset.

## Product/project evidence record

Every compliance assertion must bind:
- exact asset/product/system, manufacturer, product model and design/BOM revision;
- building use, site/geology, project phase, scope, height/occupancy and installation context;
- law/standard/part/edition/amendment and effective dates;
- authority/standards publisher, exact source URL and retrieval date;
- certificate/approval number, holder, scope, conditions, exclusions, validity and expiry;
- test report, sample/configuration, test method, lab identity/accreditation and scope;
- responsible design professional, registration/competence, discipline and sign-off scope;
- municipal submission/plan decision, inspections and occupation certificate as applicable;
- change-control mapping for any material, supplier, process, geometry, installer, site or revision change;
- evidence status (draft, current, superseded, expired, revoked, suspended, announced, invalidated, unknown);
- content digest, source revision and claim ceiling.

SANS standards are copyrighted. Record identifiers, edition, scope and the lawful access reference; do not redistribute standards text without rights.

## Design implications for CityMaker

- Do not call a construction approach “approved” because it is in an IBT spreadsheet or has a product certificate for a different configuration.
- Do not substitute a certificate across an altered material, supplier, geometry, factory/process, installer or site without checking the certificate's scope.
- Do not issue an automated compliance score. Return requirement-by-requirement states: Applicable, NotApplicable with reason, Satisfied, Blocked, Unknown, Draft, Expired, Revoked or Disputed.
- Hard statutory/technical gates remain non-compensable. Cost or productivity cannot offset missing fire, structural, building-control or safety evidence.
- Compliance evidence does not replace professional judgement or statutory authority. It makes the evidence and omissions auditable.
