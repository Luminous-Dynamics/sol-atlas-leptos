# CityMaker above-ground product-family coverage matrix v0.1

**Status:** research inventory and gap-analysis framework. No local production capacity, supplier, facility, price, material availability or compliance claim is made by this matrix.

## 1. Purpose

This matrix answers a simple but often skipped question:

> What physical products, components, equipment, skills, factories, logistics and lifecycle services are needed to build and maintain all the essential services in a city?

It connects the service-basket concept to real product families, without assuming that every product must be manufactured inside the city. Each family must be accounted for as one of:
- locally produced and independently qualified;
- produced regionally or nationally and deliverable;
- imported and supply-chain dependent;
- existing asset retained/maintained;
- recoverable/reusable from existing stocks;
- unavailable or unknown.

The focus is above-ground buildings, facilities, plant, public realm and construction/manufacturing. Below-ground utilities are represented only as boundary dependencies and interfaces.

## 3. Buildability chain and no-shortcut states

For every product family, follow the entire chain:

`service requirement -> facility / asset -> product family -> design / BOM -> process plan -> qualified capability -> qualified material -> time-compatible supply -> delivery -> installation -> inspection -> commissioning -> operations -> maintenance -> repair / replacement -> recovery`

Each link carries an owner, contract, source revision, subject identity, evidence state and receipt. An absent link remains Unknown or Blocked.

The following distinctions are mandatory:

- **Design exists** does not mean it can be manufactured.
- **Machine exists** does not mean its capability is qualified for this task.
- **Supplier exists** does not mean inventory is available.
- **Material exists** does not mean its grade or lot is qualified.
- **Inventory exists** does not mean it can be delivered in the window.
- **Delivered** does not mean accepted.
- **Installed** does not mean commissioned.
- **Commissioned** does not mean maintained.
- **Recyclable by description** does not mean recoverable at the required grade or quantity.

## 4. City capability and production-location questions

For each family ask whether capability belongs at:
- building/site-level (simple assembly, repair, small fabrication);
- neighbourhood repair/production hub;
- district/regional factory;
- national supplier/manufacturer;
- international supply chain.

Do not optimize for "make everything locally." A city needs resilient access to essential goods and repair, not autarky at any cost. Compare local production with regional/national/imported options on:
- qualified output and actual utilization;
- delivered cost and lead-time distribution;
- inventory coverage and substitutability;
- transport, energy, water and land burden;
- local skills, training and maintenance;
- failure concentration and recovery;
- social value and workforce conditions;
- ecological/lifecycle impacts.

A local factory that depends on a single imported spare part or proprietary service contract may be less resilient than a transparent regional supply route with qualified substitutes. Keep the dependency visible.

## 5. Benchmark design

### Synthetic coverage tests (E1)
At least 24 cases:
- missing product family for an essential service;
- required assembly has no process route;
- process has no qualified machine/tool capability;
- capability evidence is declared but stale/unsupported;
- material grade is incompatible;
- supply cannot meet the time window;
- delivery/receiving constraint blocks the project;
- receiving evidence missing;
- installation does not match released revision;
- commissioning receipt missing;
- maintenance skill/tool/part missing;
- replacement interface incompatible;
- critical input has no substitute;
- imported supply mistakenly counted as local productive capability;
- one lot/batch counted twice;
- material passport revoked;
- public-use rights unknown;
- one district has an essential-service gap hidden by city averages;
- coupled service dependencies (clinic, water, power, cold-chain) incomplete;
- source rights prevent public release;
- a missing upstream process prevents downstream assembly;
- stale maintenance record;
- two facilities contend for one shared resource;
- complete synthetic chain produces a traceable coverage result.

### External method comparison (E2)
- Use the ICI five pillars as an industrialization-maturity crosswalk: standardization, prefabrication, modularization, automation and digitalization.
- Use NIST task-based robot performance methods for applicable perception, mobility, dexterity and safety capabilities.
- Compare construction automation results using accepted output, quality, setup, geometric accuracy, rework, labour, safety and lifecycle boundary.
- Reproduce UN-Habitat / SDG service and public-space indicators exactly before presenting CityMaker extensions.

### Measured physical validation (E3+)
- One dwelling archetype and one clinic or school vertical, each with released requirements and independently accepted outputs.
- Track complete design-to-commissioning evidence, not only factory production.
- Replicate conventional / productized / assisted / guarded-robot processes on the same functional unit.
- Report labour by role, accepted output, defects, costs, energy, water, waste, installation, commissioning and maintenance separately.
- Field deployment requires professional review, community/worker participation, site-specific risk assessment and lawful commissioning.

## 6. Industry position and how to exceed it

2026 industrialized-construction research and survey evidence says the industry has the methods but struggles to scale them consistently:
- The ICI framework assesses five maturity pillars and was applied to 61 residential projects in Colombia and 33 in France.
- Autodesk's 2026 survey of 869 industry respondents reports 37–40% regular/standard adoption of productization, DfMA and prefabrication; among users, 84% report improved quality, 82% improved schedule performance and 76% improved cost/schedule predictability. These are respondent-reported results, not causal or independently measured effect sizes.
- NIST's robotics assessment framework decomposes task performance into perception, mobility, dexterity and safety and notes the difficulty of composing component performance into reliable assembly outcomes.
- A 2026 PRISMA review of 375 construction-robotics studies identifies barriers to real-world deployment, emphasizing that robot type, task application and human–robot collaboration must be considered together.
- A September 2026 review of 163 construction HRC studies identifies cognitive, affective, physical and acceptance/usability outcomes as distinct dimensions.
- NIOSH's 7 October 2026 bulletin stresses that robots can remove exposure to hazardous tasks while introducing risks through human–robot interaction, software, sensing and communications.
- A 2025 South African review of construction/demolition waste identifies under-reporting and omission of informal waste flows as material data-quality problems. This argues for measured custody and coverage, not a circularity percentage inferred from official totals alone.

**CityMaker should exceed current practice in reproducibility and completeness, not by claiming a higher maturity score.** Its proposed differentiator is a versioned evidence chain from service requirements to accepted and commissioned physical assets, including the people, materials, factories, logistics and maintenance needed to keep them functioning.

## 7. Owner integration

- **Sol-Atlas:** spatial service/product coverage, alternative comparison, source readiness, neighbourhood/district gaps and read-only city projection.
- **Symthaea:** design intent, process/manufacturing capability, product/facility co-design and productive closure.
- **Symtropy:** workcell, construction process, resource, physical and scenario feasibility; panel-cell benchmark.
- **Mycelix:** supply offers, inventory/production networks, physical material and energy/water flows, batch/custody/provenance and multi-party commitments.

This matrix must not instantiate a parallel product ontology, manufacturing scheduler, inventory ledger, closure theorem, safety controller, procurement authority or physical material balance.

## Claim ceiling

This is a product-family inventory and benchmarking framework. It is not proof that any particular city can locally manufacture all products, that a factory exists or is capable, that cost/safety/environmental outcomes have improved, or that all relevant community needs have been captured. Those claims require source-qualified evidence, matched physical trials and legitimate public decisions.
