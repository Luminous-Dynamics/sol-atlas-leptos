"# HDB-12 — Evidence-Bounded Historical Landscape Reconstruction v0.1\n\n## Objective\n\nValidate reconstruction package claim semantics before rendering, publication or downstream analysis. A detailed mesh is not automatically a well-supported historical model. Its elements must say whether their geometry is observed, inferred, simulated or unknown; what evidence and method produced them; what chronology and uncertainty are available; and what coordinate-access tier governs the geometry.\n\nThe approach follows the London Charter's requirements for documentation of visualisation methods, representation type, uncertainty, source evidence and interpretive decisions. It also composes with HDB-9 interval-censored chronology, HDB-11 observability, HDB-13 claim ceilings, Mycelix evidence-independence attestation and Symtropy's simulation lineage.\n\n## Epistemic geometry classes\n\n- **Observed:** geometry directly measured in a declared acquisition or field survey, with spatial reference, positional uncertainty, measurement evidence and exact method/build lineage. \"Observed\" means the geometry was measured in the present; it does not mean the object is ancient.\n- **Inferred:** geometry derived from empirical evidence and a declared method/build. It has uncertainty, source evidence, and explicit assumptions/prior dependencies. Model outputs cannot act as independent empirical support.\n- **Simulated:** geometry generated by a declared forward model or scenario, with model/build and simulation-run manifest digests and scenario uncertainty. Simulation can explore consequences; it cannot prove that the represented thing existed.\n- **Unknown:** the available evidence does not constrain defensible geometry. Unknown elements carry no geometry asset, spatial reference or geometry uncertainty pretending to be known.\n\n## Evidence and dependency binding\n\nEach evidence record carries a source-asset digest, source-lineage identity, method family, evidence kind/origin/relation, rights and a rights receipt. Each element references the exact evidence IDs used. Source-evidence dependencies must match the element's source evidence set exactly. Derived files and visualisations do not multiply independent evidence.\n\nEach observed/inferred/simulated geometry element also binds its method-build digest to a dependency entry. Simulated geometry additionally binds a simulation-manifest digest to a distinct dependency and requires at least one simulation-model/environmental-scenario dependency.\n\nThis module performs structural validation only. It does not authenticate asset bytes, source truth, rights receipts, the Mycelix/HCEA independence attestation, professional qualifications, cultural authority or the physical correctness of a geometry pipeline.\n\n## Chronology\n\nChronology is an interval, not an invented point date:\n- Bounded: both bounds and at least one direct chronometric or historical-document evidence ID.\n- Open start / open end: preserve the unknown bound explicitly.\n- Disputed: retain competing/open bounds and chronology evidence; never reverse interval bounds.\n- Unknown: no bounds or chronology source IDs.\n\nThe validator checks chronology evidence IDs resolve to supported chronological evidence. It does not infer a date from visual resemblance or typology.\n\n## Uncertainty and identifiability\n\nEvery non-unknown geometry needs a declared uncertainty representation, nominal coverage level and characteristic extent. Simulated geometry uses scenario-range uncertainty, not measurement covariance masquerading as observation accuracy.\n\nIdentifiability is explicit. A non-identifiable element requires a hypothesis-family ID and hypothesis ID, and the package must retain at least two distinct alternatives in that family. This is a minimal structural guard; it does not prove those alternatives exhaust the historical possibility space or have calibrated posterior weights.\n\n## Coordinate access\n\nIf a feature is generalized, controlled or restricted, exact geometry publication is disallowed. Public derivative maps and meshes must obey the governing custodial policy; changing the display layer does not make the underlying exact coordinates public.\n\n## HDB-12 benchmark tracks\n\n- Measured-geometry fidelity against independent check points / surveyed surfaces.\n- Geometry uncertainty coverage at 50/80/90/95% intervals, with sharpness and site-level uncertainty.\n- Evidence-removal / prior-shift sensitivity and false narrowing of uncertainty.\n- Hypothesis-set preservation when the observations are non-identifying.\n- Temporal interval coverage against independently dated evidence.\n- Landscape relationship accuracy: route/water networks, catchments, terrace-field-settlement adjacency, topology and chronological palimpsests.\n- Claim-overreach rate, simulation-to-observation false promotion and coordinate-policy violations.\n\nEvaluation must hold out whole sites/buildings/landscapes, and measured surfaces used for evaluation must not leak into training, tuning, prior setting or registration.\n\n## Negative controls\n\n- Simulation-only geometry cannot be labelled observed or inferred as empirical fact.\n- Model output cannot self-confirm.\n- Source dependency IDs must match the actual element evidence set.\n- The method-build digest on the element must match a declared method dependency.\n- Simulation manifest digest must match a declared manifest dependency.\n- Reversed chronology bounds fail.\n- Unknown geometry must not carry asset/spatial geometry.\n- Non-identifiable reconstructions must preserve at least two alternatives.\n- Restricted exact geometry cannot be published.\n- Missing rights/source/lineage/uncertainty dependencies fail closed.\n\n## Claim ceiling\n\nA report disposition of `structurally_qualified` means only that the submitted package conforms to these typed structural rules. It does not mean the reconstructed landscape is historically true, archaeologically significant, physically accurate, licensed for every use or authorized for publication/fieldwork. It does not turn a simulated landscape into evidence of a past state.\n\n"
