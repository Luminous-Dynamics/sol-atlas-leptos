# City Morph Safety — Research Brief v0.1

**Programme:** Luminous Habitats / CityMaker  
**Parent:** [CITY-MAKER-001 #549](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/sol-atlas-leptos/issues/549)  
**Execution issue:** [CITY-MORPH-SAFE-001 #591](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/sol-atlas-leptos/issues/591)  
**Status:** research specification; no field performance or building approval claimed  
**Context:** South Africa first; initial climate/site reference is Johannesburg/Highveld, not a parcel-specific design

## Executive thesis

The programme should optimize neither for the appearance of a fortress nor for the highest density of security equipment. It should co-design a **beautiful, legible, inclusive and resilient built environment** whose safety properties can be measured.

The unit of analysis is not only the dwelling. It is the journey from private room to front door, shared courtyard, street, transport, school, clinic, workplace and public space—across day/night conditions, service disruptions, construction phases and life-safety events.

A “safe” result is a scoped, time-bounded claim backed by relevant evidence. It is never a universal property inferred from a rendering, map, camera count, gate count, model confidence or synthetic benchmark.

## 1. Why South Africa needs a more complete benchmark

Stats SA's 2025/26 Governance, Public Safety and Justice Survey reports:
- 80.7% of people aged 16+ felt safe walking alone in their neighbourhood during the day;
- 37.9% felt safe walking alone when it is dark;
- in Gauteng, 73.1% reported feeling unsafe walking alone after dark;
- housebreaking victimisation is materially under-reported; fewer than half of affected households report some or all incidents to SAPS.

These figures are national/provincial survey statistics, not a local Johannesburg street baseline. Perceived safety, survey-estimated victimisation and police-recorded crime are different measures with different denominators and reporting biases. Preserve them separately.

**Design implication:** neighbourhood safety must improve people's ability to use their environment, not only protect private property. Day/night experience, safe mobility, service access, life safety and the risk transferred to adjacent areas must all be measured.

## 2. What the evidence says—and what it does not

Widmark (2026), a residential CPTED systematic review/meta-analysis, included 31 studies and pooled 29. Its pooled result suggested a statistically significant reduction in property crime across included interventions, but heterogeneity was very high (I² above 90%). Average violent-crime effects were not statistically significant. The reported approximately 32% pooled property-crime reduction is **not** an expected outcome for Johannesburg, a guaranteed effect, or a target to design toward. The review itself calls for stronger comparable evaluations and context reporting.

The review examined several intervention classes, including access control, target hardening, greening and street lighting. It also found crime displacement/diffusion was examined in many, but not all, included studies. That makes displacement accounting a required benchmark feature.

The DHS/CSIR Neighbourhood Planning and Design Guide (Red Book) is the South African foundation for neighbourhood design. Its scope already includes neighbourhood layout, public open space, housing and social facilities, transportation, water, sanitation, stormwater, electricity, safe communities and universal design. CityMaker should consume and operationalize this local guidance, not produce a competing generic manual.

Construction robotics remains less widely deployed in construction than in manufacturing. A June 2026 systematic review assessed 375 construction robotics studies and identified real-world deployment barriers. A separate 2026 AEC human-robot collaboration review of 52 studies notes that short-term experiments and convenience participants are common; dynamic construction environments require better task, temporal and field-context evaluation. Therefore, the first robotics win should be repeatable, instrumented, off-site production and inspection—not an autonomous robot roaming an occupied neighbourhood.

## 3. Paradigm shifts

| Old default | Luminous Habitat approach | How to test it |
|---|---|---|
| Secure perimeter equals safe neighbourhood | Whole-journey safety, home-to-service and public realm | Route audits, day/night experience, accessible network continuity |
| More cameras means more security | Proportionate, accountable guardianship; visibility, active frontage, maintenance and resident choice | Separate detection, response, crime, victimisation, privacy and perceived-safety outcomes |
| One defensive typology | Matched portfolio of gated, courtyard, active-frontage, polycentric, retrofit and maintain-existing forms | Same population, budget, service requirements, time window and data coverage |
| Static architectural masterplan | Phased living system with service and construction continuity | Stress cases across build phases, outages and maintenance |
| One weighted “safety score” | Non-compensatory life-safety/rights gates plus Pareto outcomes | Independent hard-gate oracle and exposed trade-offs |
| AI proposes a design and approves itself | AI proposes; specialist solvers and qualified humans verify within explicit scope | False-acceptance tests, independent calculations and claim-tier controls |
| Bespoke construction | Design for fabrication, assembly, inspection, repair and disassembly | Matched human, assisted and robot workcell benchmarks |
| Fortress aesthetics imported from elsewhere | Place-rooted South African architecture and co-design | Paid resident/artist/architect review, usability and cultural-fit evidence |

Additional architectural research thesis: create a **regional design grammar**, not a style kit. Parametric families should adapt roof, shade, courtyard, veranda, material, threshold, landscape and circulation to actual climate, local craft, site ecology and resident-stated practices. Do not infer cultural preferences from demographic proxies. Aesthetic quality, belonging, perceived safety and crime outcomes are related but distinct research variables.

## 4. South African architectural design envelope

For the initial Johannesburg/Highveld reference scenario, investigate—not assume:
- orientation, winter solar gain, summer shade and glare;
- thermal mass, insulation, ventilation and condensation;
- stormwater, intense rainfall, hail and local flood pathways;
- water harvesting, treatment, storage, safe reuse and potable-water separation;
- solar PV, batteries, load management and manual/offline control;
- indigenous/climate-appropriate planting, shade and biodiversity;
- locally available, qualified stone, timber, earth, steel, masonry and cementitious products;
- accessibility, fire separation, evacuation, emergency access and maintainability.

Every real parcel requires a current legal and site register: title/deed restrictions, CoJ zoning and land use, servitudes, geotechnical and environmental constraints, building-plan approval, current applicable SANS 10400 provisions, fire requirements, NHBRC scope/enrolment and any Agrément assessment for innovative systems. Check the current edition/status of each applicable standard when the actual site and building class are known. Never treat a draft Bill, guideline or software rule as enacted approval.

### Materials and construction

Use a method portfolio, not a technology ideology:
- conventional site-built masonry/concrete/steel/timber;
- repair, retrofit and adaptive reuse;
- panelized and open-building systems;
- precast and prefabricated assemblies;
- timber/hybrid systems where moisture, fire, durability and supply evidence supports them;
- robotic cutting, machining, drilling, fastening, welding, handling, inspection and assembly;
- bounded additive construction only where material, reinforcement, interlayer bond, durability, dimensional acceptance and inspection criteria are established.

Concrete printing is not a universal replacement for conventional building. Its value is application-dependent, and printed geometry alone does not establish reinforcement, structural capacity, fire performance or durability.

## 5. Comparative benchmark design

Compare six candidate urban/architectural archetypes under matched functional requirements:

1. **Controlled-entry/gated baseline** — a reference for current market security practice, with costs and externalities included.
2. **Courtyard cluster** — layered public-to-private transitions, common garden and legible shared space.
3. **Active-frontage mixed-use block** — connected pedestrian network, inhabited edges and daily destinations.
4. **Polycentric neighbourhood** — distributed services, several safe routes and local activity nodes.
5. **Retrofit/repair-first** — upgrade existing dwellings, routes, lighting, public space and service continuity.
6. **Maintain-existing/no-build** — counterfactual to prevent unnecessary construction being counted as success.

Freeze the study boundary, population, household mix, service-capacity assumptions, intervention budget, staffing/maintenance resources, exposure period, transport supply, climate profile and data coverage. Include the adjacent-area buffer and record spillovers. A reduction inside the pilot area is not a success if crime, risk or exclusion is transferred outside it.

Do not preselect an urban form. Report Pareto sets and scenario-specific results. Include affordability, displacement and public access as hard review dimensions, not small weighted penalties.

## 6. Measurement model

### A. Victimisation and recorded crime
- offence-specific event counts and rates with an explicit exposure denominator;
- survey-estimated victimisation and reporting/non-reporting;
- SAPS-recorded incidents and station/geographic coverage;
- severity and repeat victimisation where appropriately available;
- spatial, temporal, target and offence displacement; diffusion of benefits;
- source date, revision, resolution, uncertainty and missing-data status.

### B. Lived experience
- day/night perceived safety on named route types and journeys;
- walking, cycling, waiting, trip completion and route avoidance;
- public-space use by time;
- cohort-level differences where legal, ethical and statistically valid;
- consent, non-identifying publication and paid participation.

### C. CPTED and public realm
- accessible route continuity and wayfinding;
- visible and overlooked spaces, while protecting privacy;
- active frontage and usable destinations;
- access threshold legibility and proportional control;
- lighting coverage, glare, uniformity, outage time and repair time under qualified criteria;
- maintenance, vegetation, abandoned-space and obstruction measures.

### D. Life safety
- fire/egress review and accessible evacuation;
- responder access, address legibility and route availability;
- medical response access;
- outage conditions and manual fallbacks;
- flood, heat, storm, smoke and construction-phase scenarios;
- qualified drill or physical test evidence where appropriate.

### E. Lifecycle and justice
- total cost of ownership per home and person served;
- staffing, energy, maintenance and replacement burden;
- embodied/operational carbon and water;
- accessibility, affordability, local employment and local repair capacity;
- displacement, public-space loss, exclusion and risk transfer.

Keep all these outcomes independently visible. A beautiful place can still be unsafe; a low-crime place can still be hostile, inaccessible or unaffordable.

## 7. Benchmark tiers and proposed gates

**E0 — proposal:** conceptual design, assumed parameters and unverified claims.  
**E1 — synthetic:** frozen finite corpus plus independent Rust oracle.  
**E2 — simulation:** external solver result within a declared, independently validated scope.  
**E3 — physical:** instrumented component or mock-up with calibrated measurement and traceable as-built identity.  
**E4 — field:** supervised, consented pilot with independent inspection and qualified professional review.  
**E5 — sustained:** longitudinal operation, maintenance, replication and external review.

A tier belongs to a specific claim, version, place and time. It does not confer a global safety certificate.

### P0 — mandatory digital/integrity goals
- 100% deterministic replay under frozen deterministic fixture inputs.
- 100% of safety claims bind source, vintage, geographic resolution, denominator, uncertainty, scope and reviewer role.
- 100% of missing, stale or unsupported evidence preserved as Unknown/limited.
- Zero false acceptance across all seeded life-safety and data-integrity negative controls.
- 100% detection of seeded unit, boundary, stale source, denominator, geometry and profile mismatch.
- No rendering, model confidence, camera count or synthetic pass can establish real-world safety or code compliance.

### P1 — outcome improvements
Freeze numeric targets only after local baseline and statistical/power analysis. Preregister:
- measurable improvement in day/night perceived safety and trip completion;
- reduction in selected offence-specific victimisation;
- no meaningful adverse spillover to adjacent areas;
- lower maintenance/repair time and service downtime;
- no reduction in accessibility, public access, fire/egress, affordability, privacy or ecological performance.

Use matched comparison areas and longitudinal designs. Where feasible, use quasi-experimental or ethically acceptable stepped-wedge methods. Publish confidence intervals, uncertainty, exclusions and denominator changes, not just percentage improvements.

12. construction detour creating access deficit;
13. emergency vehicle cannot reach dwelling;
14. inaccessible evacuation route;
15. grid outage disabling lighting;
16. flood disconnects essential service route;
17. subgroup safety perceptions diverge;
18. cheap option increases lifecycle costs;
19. camera detection rises without safety gain;
20. controlled access reduces public access;
21. intervention shifts crime next door;
22. new barrier creates step-free access failure;
23. material/installation defect;
24. response service unavailable;
25. conflicting household needs;
26. uncertain site risk and value-of-information;

29. low reported crime hides non-reporting;
30. private security harms community frontage.

Independent oracle recomputes source identity, denominators, evidence status, hard constraints and comparisons. It is kept separate from candidate-generation code. A test suite proves only software behavior against its test corpus; it does not validate a physical building.

## 9. Four-system contract

### Sol-Atlas
Owns source-bounded geography, route/service projections, urban form, distributional comparisons, public-realm data coverage and Pareto presentation. It does not issue building approval or safety certification.

### Symthaea
Owns intent translation, explicit requirements, alternative generation, conflict explanation, uncertainty, value-of-information and experiment design. It must abstain when required site/legal/engineering/consent evidence is missing. No self-certification.

### Symtropy
Owns exact geometric/spatial projections, supported physical/resource/process semantics and deterministic evaluation. Specialist structural, fire, wind, seismic, geotechnical, energy and daylight solvers remain separate validated adapters. Its general rigid-body physics must not be described as structural engineering.

### Mycelix
Owns consent, authorized decisions, provenance, custody, inspection/approval records, correction, revocation and disputes. A signed record does not itself become a permit, professional sign-off, property title or robot command.

### Robotics
Compose existing timber platform and construction capability work. Begin with off-site robotic fabrication, measurement, material handling, inspection and supervised assembly. Do not start with autonomous robots operating unsupervised in occupied residential/public space. Bind every task to exact robot, tool, calibration, workspace, material, capability envelope, interlocks, approval role and recovery path.

## 10. First physical and digital demonstrators

**Digital demonstrator A — Highveld Keep (single dwelling):** courtyard residence, shaded covered circulation, great hall/family space, private sleeping/work zones, accessible arrival, service/delivery threshold, maintainable perimeter, measured daylight/thermal/ventilation models, water-energy systems, fire/egress and outage cases. It is a reference design study, not a buildable-for-permit package.

**Digital demonstrator B — Living Hamlet (courtyard cluster):** 4–12 synthetic dwellings around a shared courtyard, connected to a walkable street, local service node and public space. Compare six urban archetypes and disruption scenarios.

**Physical demonstrator C — guarded fabrication/inspection cell:** one small timber or panel assembly process. Start with metrology, scanning, fixture validation, dry-run, supervised handling and independent inspection. Advance to cutting/fastening only after physical risk assessment, engineered guarding, safety-rated controls, commissioning and qualified operator training.

Robots should specialize by task: fixed arms/gantries for repeatable fabrication, mobile carts for logistics, scanning devices for metrology, and qualified human operators for heavy lifts, exceptions and sign-off. The benchmark is accepted, inspected output per total resource and risk—not robot autonomy percentage.

## 11. Interoperability and evidence formats

Prefer standards-based exchange where suitable:
- IFC 4.3 / ISO 16739-1:2024 for built asset and infrastructure information exchange;
- IDS 1.0 for machine-readable alphanumeric exchange requirements;
- explicit geometry checks separate from IDS property checks;
- versioned source, unit, coordinate-frame, uncertainty and validity metadata;
- RICS whole-life carbon methodology where applicable, with South African project boundary and local factor provenance recorded.

Do not create another universal building ontology if the current IFC/ISO, jurisdictional, discipline and project contracts can be mapped. Use a narrow adapter and preserve source authority.
