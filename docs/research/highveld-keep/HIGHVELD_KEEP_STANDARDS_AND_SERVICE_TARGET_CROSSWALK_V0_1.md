## 1. Why this crosswalk exists

Highveld Keep already has a design brief, multiple spatial families, an AEC workflow comparison and a specialist-solver strategy. This appendix connects that work to the building-performance, health, sustainability and whole-life frameworks that owners, designers, certifiers and residents may actually use.

The frameworks below have different purposes. They must not be collapsed into one score or described as interchangeable certifications:

- **South African regulation** defines legal requirements and approval pathways. It is mandatory where applicable.
- **Voluntary rating systems** provide structured performance and market comparators.
- **Technical standards** define methods or requirements for particular domains.
- **Health guidance** informs risk management; it is not automatically a building-code limit.
- **Project targets** are hypotheses to test against a real household, site, climate, budget and professional review.

The purpose is not to design for the greatest number of certificates. It is to deliver safe, comfortable and resilient household services, with claims that survive independent scrutiny.

## 2. Standards and rating-system map

| Framework | What it is useful for | How Highveld Keep should use it | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **South African National Building Regulations and SANS 10400, including Part XA (2021 edition)** | Statutory building-regulation and energy-usage requirements where applicable | Establish a version-pinned legal requirements register by building classification, municipality, submission date and applicable deemed-to-satisfy / rational-design route; have the architect and relevant professionals confirm applicability | A project checklist or software result is not municipal approval. Check the current consolidated regulations, referenced SANS editions, amendments and local authority interpretation at design submission |
| **NHBRC requirements and enrolment pathway** | South African home-building and housing-construction quality / enrolment context | Determine whether and how the eventual project and builder are in scope; capture the relevant enrolment, inspection and warranty evidence | Enrolment is not a substitute for the full professional design, municipal approval or commissioning |
| **GBCSA Green Star SA** | South African sustainability rating framework across management, indoor environmental quality, energy, transport, water, materials, land use/ecology, emissions and socio-economic outcomes | Use the current applicable residential / building tool as a local sustainability cross-check; identify the project type, tool version, evidence requirements and auditor from the beginning | Verify typology eligibility and current tool version. A design-stage score or checklist is not an awarded rating |
| **EDGE** | Resource-efficiency certification using comparison against a defined local baseline | Use its energy, water and embodied-energy-in-materials savings structure as a quantified design comparator; model the same functional service and baseline assumptions | EDGE savings are modelled against its applicable baseline and methodology, not universal percentage reductions against every existing home |
| **Passive House / PHI criteria** | High-performance envelope, heating and cooling/dehumidification demand, airtightness, summer comfort, humidity and renewable primary energy | Use the current PHI criteria and PHPP for a stringent whole-envelope comparator; report the heating-demand or heating-load path, cooling/dehumidification allowance, n50, overheating/humidity, and PER class separately | The commonly cited 15 kWh/(m²·year) heating-demand path is not the whole standard. Cooling/dehumidification includes climate/use allowances, and the summer comfort and moisture criteria remain material. Do not optimize airtightness without a qualified ventilation and moisture strategy |
| **ASHRAE Standard 55 (2023)** | Thermal environmental conditions for human occupancy | Use the selected method and its applicability limits to define operative-temperature / air-speed / humidity / radiant-environment evaluation | It is a thermal-comfort method/standard, not a structural, medical or emergency-safety certification |
| **ASHRAE Standard 62.2 (2025)** | Ventilation and acceptable indoor-air-quality requirements for low-rise residential buildings within its scope | Use as a residential ventilation reference where the dwelling and jurisdictional pathway make it applicable; declare local code precedence and system scope | It does not replace pollutant-specific source control, filtration selection, commissioning or local legal requirements |
| **WHO air-quality and indoor-air guidance** | Health-based pollutant guidance, including particulate matter and selected indoor pollutants | Maintain pollutant-specific measurement and source-control plans. The WHO 2021 annual PM2.5 guideline of 5 μg/m³ may be shown as an external-air-quality reference point; indoor interpretation must be explicit. Include relevant indoor guidance for formaldehyde and radon where applicable | WHO ambient-air guideline values are not automatically enforceable indoor limits or a substitute for South African occupational / building / public-health rules |
| **RICS Whole Life Carbon Assessment, 2nd edition** | Consistent whole-life carbon accounting for buildings and infrastructure | Declare lifecycle modules, study period, quantities, EPD quality, replacement cycles, operational energy assumptions, biogenic-carbon treatment and Module D separately | Do not compare carbon numbers with different boundaries, service lives, grid factors or EPD quality as if they were equivalent |
| **Living Building Challenge 4.1** | Performance-based regenerative framework covering Place, Water, Energy, Health + Happiness, Materials, Equity and Beauty | Use as a stretch design-review lens for regenerative performance, place, equity and beauty; identify which imperatives are testable and which require evidence over time | It is a demanding voluntary programme, not a substitute for local approvals or a default minimum for every household |

## 3. Candidate performance ambitions

The following are **proposed design and research targets**, not current Highveld Keep results, universal requirements, guarantees or substitutes for the applicable standards. Freeze numeric acceptance criteria only after the resident brief, site, climate package, baseline home and functional units are fixed.

### 3.1 Energy and carbon

1. Compare against a **competent, code-compliant local reference dwelling** delivering the same household functions, occupancy schedule, comfort expectations, hot-water service and appliance use.
2. Use EDGE Advanced's published threshold structure as a design challenge where eligible: at least 40% modeled energy savings, alongside at least 20% water and embodied-energy-in-materials savings against its applicable baseline. Do not describe this as achieved or assume certification eligibility.
3. Report annual delivered energy in kWh/year, energy intensity in kWh/m²/year, peak kW, end-use breakdown, self-consumption, imports/exports, storage losses and backup-fuel use. State conditioned / unconditioned floor-area boundaries and household occupancy.
4. Report upfront and whole-life GWP separately, using a declared RICS-aligned boundary, study period, replacement assumptions and electricity scenarios. Avoid a net-zero claim based only on annual PV generation; include seasonal mismatch, storage, imports, export treatment and backup.

**Stretch hypothesis:** a dwelling with a substantially lower demand profile and a feasible, maintainable renewable-energy system should outperform the local baseline without relying on routine occupant discomfort, unsafe load shedding or uncosted battery replacement.

### 3.2 Water

1. Report potable withdrawal by use, non-potable withdrawal by source, rainwater capture, reuse delivered by approved end use, overflow, treatment rejects, leakage and storage losses.
2. Report litres/person/day and litres per delivered household service, with household size and service definition explicit.
3. Maintain separate, physically protected potable and non-potable networks. No reuse claim may pass without intended-use-specific water-quality evidence, backflow protection and professional / municipal acceptance.
4. Stress-test municipal low pressure, supply interruption, depleted storage, sensor failure, treatment failure, contamination challenge and drought restrictions. Show the hours/days until each resident-defined service floor is breached.
5. Compare reuse against no-reuse and low-water-demand baselines. Count treatment energy, consumables, residuals, maintenance labour, cross-connection risk and lifecycle cost.

**Stretch hypothesis:** minimize potable water for non-potable tasks while keeping essential potable reserves and safe sanitation non-negotiable. Storage autonomy is a site- and household-specific result, not a universal number.

### 3.3 Thermal comfort, ventilation and indoor air

1. Use a declared ASHRAE 55 method / applicable local criteria and report occupied-hour comfort distribution by room, season and resident group, not only a whole-house average.
2. Report operative temperature, air temperature, mean radiant temperature or qualified proxy, humidity, air speed, solar exposure, ventilation state and system energy.
3. Report both annual performance and time-resolved heatwave, hot-night, cold-night and power-outage trajectories. Include time to degradation, service-floor breach and stable recovery.
4. Commission ventilation and report outdoor-air delivery, filter type and pressure drop, fan energy, noise, humidity/condensation, maintenance state and pollutant-specific outcomes.
5. Keep PM2.5, PM10, CO2, NO2, formaldehyde, radon, combustion products and moisture/mould indicators distinct. A low CO2 reading does not establish that every pollutant is controlled; a filter CADR does not establish that required outdoor air is supplied.

**Candidate internal design goal:** at least 95% of occupied hours inside the resident-approved comfort envelope in the declared normal-operation study, with every remaining exceedance disclosed and analyzed. This is a project ambition, not an ASHRAE 55 certification threshold. Outage and extreme-event outcomes must be separately reported.

### 3.4 Daylight, acoustic quality and human experience

- Daylight autonomy, useful daylight, glare probability / discomfort, view access and electric-light energy should be reported independently.
- Acoustic privacy and noise should be measured at the room / boundary relevant to the task; don't infer acoustic performance from plan separation alone.
- Residents should be able to express incomparable or conflicting preferences. Keep accessibility, privacy, cultural meaning, beauty and sensory preferences as visible dimensions; do not hide them inside a universal scalar score.
- Conduct blind review with residents and appropriate local architects, artists, craftspeople and access professionals. Record disagreement and cohort-specific outcomes.

### 3.5 Resilience and essential service

Model and report separate service trajectories for electricity, potable water, sanitation, hot water, ventilation / air cleaning, thermal comfort, cooking, food preservation, communications and access.

For each scenario report:
- initial reserve and service demand;
- time to degradation and time to first service-floor breach;
- unmet service by task and affected resident group;
- manual fallback and local operability;
- repair access, replacement time, spare-part delay and re-verification;
- stable recovery after restoration, including recurrence;
- model uncertainty and any excluded or out-of-domain interval.

A home is not “independent” merely because it has PV, batteries, tanks or a dashboard. Independence is a measured duration and service scope under a declared disturbance trace.

### 3.6 Materials, repair and adaptation

- Compare structure and envelope pathways on equivalent function, durability, fire/moisture/pest exposure, sourcing, embodied carbon, maintenance and repair.
- Capture product identity, manufacturer, material grade, batch/lot, EPD, approval status, local availability, lead time, substitute compatibility and end-of-life route.
- Measure fabrication first-pass yield, dimensional/tolerance acceptance, rework, installation hours, inspection defects and waste.
- Test at least one future household-change scenario and one component substitution. All dependent calculations and approvals must be invalidated and repeated where the change affects them.
- Use open formats and reversible interfaces; avoid a single vendor becoming the only party able to diagnose, service or export the home.

## 4. Required benchmark design

Run three distinct comparison programmes; do not mix their outputs.

### A. Design and workflow comparison

Use the same resident brief, synthetic or surveyed site, legal envelope, climate inputs, budget boundary, output maturity and time budget across:

- professional architect-led workflow with qualified specialist consultants;
- Autodesk Forma + Revit workflow at the exact feature / regional availability;
- TestFit or another suitable generative-planning workflow;
- parametric authoring plus openBIM and independent specialist tools;
- Luminous orchestration using resident intent, Sol-Atlas context, Symtropy coordination, qualified external solvers and Mycelix evidence receipts.

Measure human setup/correction/review time; requirement retention; number and quality of feasible alternatives; hard-constraint violations; unresolved unknowns; semantic/export loss; geometry and revision consistency; quantity-takeoff accuracy; and evidence completeness.

### B. Engineering / operational model comparison

Use specialist tools for the domains they already serve. Pin exact releases and model versions.

- EnergyPlus / OpenStudio: whole-building energy and supported heat/moisture systems.
- Modelica Buildings: dynamic plant, thermal stores and control-system behavior where applicable.
- BOPTEST: matched building-control policy comparisons on the available or a separately qualified test case.
- OpenFOAM or other validated CFD: airflow / local courtyard analysis when the question requires it.
- Independent engineering methods and qualified consultants: structure, fire, moisture, acoustics, hydraulics, electrical and accessibility.

Do not count a wrapper around the same solver as an independent physics implementation. Validate each adapter against its own reference cases and calibration envelope.

### C. Physical product / dwelling comparison

Only progress after independent design review, site diligence, safety review and measurement-method preflight.

Start with a low-consequence unoccupied test cell and instrumented components. Compare independent, shared-utility and hybrid/local-autonomy service architectures under identical exogenous traces. Move to supervised site and occupied evaluation only with professional, legal, statutory, privacy and resident-consent gates satisfied.

## 5. Search and evidence metrics

| Question | Metric | Initial acceptance rule |
|---|---|---|
| Did the process preserve resident intent? | Requirement coverage, hard-constraint retention, preference lineage | 100% traceability in frozen synthetic fixtures; no silent rewrite |
| Did it invent feasibility? | False-feasible rate | 0 false PASS in seeded adversarial corpus |
| Did it handle uncertainty? | Calibration / coverage, out-of-domain detection, abstention correctness | Unknown or out-of-domain remains unknown / blocked |
| Are comparisons fair? | Identical input, scenario, objective and budget digests | 100% paired runs bind the same frozen inputs and external traces |
| Is search genuinely better? | Exact tiny-case Pareto reference, hypervolume, IGD+, GD+, epsilon, regret, feasibility rate | Report only supported metrics; use independent exact fronts for tiny cases |
| Did the workflow save effort? | Human minutes, wall time, specialist calls, failed evaluations, rework | Report full costs and distributions; no software-runtime-only claim |
| Is the model credible? | Independent analytical cases, solver convergence, residuals, calibration and held-out errors | Model-specific acceptance tolerances pre-registered before result review |
| Is the physical service better? | Metered energy/water/air/thermal outcomes, uncertainty, uptime and recovery | Matched incumbent and independently admitted instruments required |
| Is the building adaptable? | Repair, replacement, inspection and change-impact time/cost | Measure actual mock-up / field tasks; do not infer from BIM metadata |
| Does it improve lived experience? | Task completion, accessibility, comprehension, privacy, satisfaction and disagreement | Paid / consented task-based studies; report each cohort and conflicts |

For stochastic search, preserve the existing Highveld protocol: fixed published seeds, matched candidate and high-fidelity evaluation budgets, training/tuning/validation/final calls all counted, and exact high-fidelity re-evaluation of every promoted candidate. Hypervolume reference points and objective normalization must be predeclared. Do not publish IGD+, GD+ or epsilon unless an independently defensible reference front exists.

## 6. Evidence and authority gates

Every claim is scoped to an exact design revision, site, occupancy, scenario, tool version, input digest, output, validation envelope and responsible authority.

- E0: concept / synthetic assumptions.
- E1: frozen synthetic corpus and independent checker.
- E2: version-pinned specialist solver result inside its independently validated scope.
- E3: calibrated physical component / mock-up evidence.
- E4: supervised real-site pilot with resident consent and professional / statutory review.
- E5: sustained operation, maintenance and independent external replication.

Keep these distinctions explicit:

```
design intent       != geometry verified
geometry verified   != site feasible
site feasible       != code approved
solver converged    != model validated
model validated     != physical performance observed
physical component  != occupied dwelling outcome
provenance receipt  != professional / statutory approval
```

No build, purchase, energization, occupancy or device-actuation authority is conferred by this crosswalk.

## 7. Immediate decisions and work sequence

1. Keep Johannesburg / Highveld as the regional reference only until a site is selected.
2. Confirm the household task brief and hard requirements before assigning a universal floor-area, budget or autonomy target.
3. Resolve the site, title/servitudes, survey, geotechnical and municipal authority envelope with qualified local professionals.
4. Acquire and qualify a rights-cleared weather ensemble: typical year, recent actual year, hot-tail / heatwave and cold events, future climate, and later paired courtyard measurements. Climate.OneBuilding catalogue presence alone does not grant redistribution rights or establish representativeness.
5. Set the code-compliant local reference dwelling and functional-equivalence boundary before calculating savings.
6. Run design workflow comparisons, then one qualified EnergyPlus zone / envelope reference case, then the room-scale unoccupied service bench.
7. Treat measured results, not the render or the optimizer's preferred candidate, as the basis for promotion.

## 8. Johannesburg operating context and certification applicability — 17 November 2026

### 8.1 Water restrictions are a live stress case, not a permanent design assumption

Johannesburg Water's Level 1 restrictions are published for **1 September 2026 through 31 March 2027**. During this period, watering gardens and filling swimming pools is prohibited from 06:00 to 18:00, and hosepipe washing of paved areas / driveways is not permitted.

Design implications for this reference dwelling:

- Include this period as a named, date-bounded operational stress case in water-demand and pool/water-feature experiments.
- Keep a pool, rill, pond, evaporative cooler or irrigation loop discretionary. None may draw down a resident-defined essential potable-water reserve or displace drinking, hygiene or sanitation floors.
- Model actual withdrawal, make-up volume, evaporation, backwash, overflow and treatment reject separately. A covered or recirculating pool is not water-neutral.
- Compare against a competent no-pool / no-water-feature baseline. Do not claim the restriction is satisfied merely because filling is scheduled outside the restricted daytime hours.
- Refresh municipal restrictions and supply-condition notices at the time of any future site design or operation; this September–March notice must not be treated as a permanent annual entitlement.

Source: [Johannesburg Water — Level 1 water restrictions, 15 September 2026](https://www.johannesburgwater.co.za/blog/2026/09/15/level-1-water-restrictions-are-here-lets-make-every-drop-count/).

### 8.2 South African energy policy: separate operative requirements from draft policy

The current design baseline remains the applicable National Building Regulations and SANS 10400-XA requirements, with the exact submission route and edition confirmed by the registered professional and local authority for the project.

The Government Gazette of **30 October 2026** published Notice 4221 of 2026 as a **draft** National Energy Efficiency Strategy 2025–35 for comment. Its discussion of future policy, building-energy codes and Energy Performance Certificates must not be treated as an enacted amendment. The draft also describes the existing EPC mandate as applying to specified larger categories (including private buildings above 2,000 m² and government buildings above 1,000 m² in specified use classes); do not assume that an EPC mandate applies to a single-family home. Verify the operative EPC regulations and scope separately.

Sources:
- [Government Gazette 55638, Notice 4221 of 2026 — Draft National Energy Efficiency Strategy](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202610/55638gen4221.pdf).
- [IEA — SANS 10400-XA:2021 policy record](https://www.iea.org/policies/8412-sans-10400-xa2021-national-building-regulations-energy-usage-in-buildings).

### 8.3 Green Star applicability and cost need an early eligibility check

GBCSA's current public materials list a **Green Star New Build V2 pilot**, existing Green Star New Buildings and Major Refurbishments tools, and a specific **Multi Unit Residential (MUR) V1** tool. The MUR V1 technical manual has a material eligibility gate: a qualifying development must include **three or more dwelling units**, common property/shared services or infrastructure, and a governance scheme (or equivalent management entity). A one-off detached Highveld Keep dwelling does **not** meet that MUR V1 development criterion by itself. The New Build V2 brochure describes a pilot, but the existence of a pilot and its fee schedule do not establish that a single detached house is eligible.

Before choosing a rating target:
1. treat MUR V1 as inapplicable to a standalone single-dwelling study unless the wider development independently satisfies its unit-count, common-property and governance criteria;
2. obtain written confirmation from GBCSA of the exact tool and building typology accepted for the proposed project, especially whether a detached single-family home can enter the New Build V2 pilot or another route;
3. confirm the current technical manual, evidence requirements, accredited-professional requirements and registration stage;
4. compare certification fees and the cost of independent assessment against the value of certification for this one dwelling;
5. preserve the rating tool as a design-review comparator even when formal certification is not proportionate.

The published 2026 brochure lists New Build V2 pilot fees of R205,000 (member) / R246,000 (non-member), VAT included; the V1 New Buildings and Major Refurbishments fee band below 1,500 m² is R78,890 / R99,000. These are published fees, not a quote for Highveld Keep, and do not establish eligibility or all professional/consultant costs.

Sources:
- [GBCSA — rating tools](https://www.gbcsa.org.za/rating-tools/).
- [GBCSA — 2026 Certification Pricing Brochure](https://www.gbcsa.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026-GBCSA-Certification-Pricing-Brochure-20260212-_f_pdf.pdf).
- [GBCSA — tool and technical-resource index](https://gbcsa.zendesk.com/hc/en-us).
- [GBCSA — Green Star SA Multi Unit Residential V1 technical manual, Eligibility Criterion 5](https://api.gbcsa.org.za/resources/download/250): three-or-more units, shared/common property and governance requirements.
- [Passive House Institute — Criteria for Buildings (current published compact criteria PDF)](https://passivehouse.com/downloads/03_building_criteria_en.pdf): heating demand/load, cooling plus dehumidification allowance, airtightness and PER class.

### 8.4 EDGE Advanced is a project-specific modeled comparator

GBCI's current EDGE certification guidance says a project is designated EDGE Advanced when it achieves at least **40% predicted energy savings**, plus at least **20% predicted water savings** and **20% predicted embodied-energy savings in materials**, relative to the applicable EDGE baseline.

These are modeled certification thresholds using EDGE's methodology and baseline. They are not universal savings against every existing Johannesburg dwelling and are not post-occupancy measured performance. For Highveld Keep, use the thresholds as a stretch comparator only after:
- confirming the residential project/site eligibility and current EDGE version;
- freezing the applicable baseline, occupancy, energy end uses, water fixtures, materials and functional unit;
- recording all assumptions, substitutions and model version;
- separately measuring actual operational results later.

Source: [GBCI — EDGE Certification](https://edge.gbci.org/certification?language=en).

## 9. Claim boundary

This appendix is a research crosswalk. It does not establish current legal compliance, eligibility for any certification, product or instrument qualification, indoor-air safety, structural/fire performance, actual energy/water savings, cost, comfort, resilience or human benefit. All quantitative ambitions are proposed comparison targets and must be frozen only after their context and baseline are justified.
