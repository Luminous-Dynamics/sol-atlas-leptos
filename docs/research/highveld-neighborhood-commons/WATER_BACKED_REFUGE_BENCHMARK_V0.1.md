# Integrated water-backed thermal refuge benchmark v0.1

**Study ID:** HIGHVELD-WATER-BACKED-REFUGE-001  
**Parent programmes:** HIGHVELD-POOL-WATER-THERMAL-001 / HIGHVELD-THERMAL-SERVICE-REFUGE-001  
**Maturity:** BENCHMARK_PROTOCOL_NOT_EXECUTED  
**Climate context:** Johannesburg / Highveld; no site or occupant data admitted

## 1. Paradigm shift: a water feature becomes a thermal-service subsystem

A pool or rill is not itself a cooling system. The useful engineering object is the complete heat-and-service path:

**occupied zone → isolated heat exchanger → finite water/PCM thermal store → qualified regeneration sinks → measured night recovery → next-day service.**

Outdoor water features are a separate microclimate intervention and require their own dry-shade controls, spatial transects and water/air-quality measurements. Water in a pool must not be treated as an infinite cold reservoir; a radiator or water-fed ceiling must not be assumed to cool the room without a metered building-side heat path.

The proposed *water-backed climate refuge* combines deep solar shade, radiant surfaces, a closed-loop water thermal buffer, optional ground and night-sky regeneration, and outdoor-air ventilation only when air quality, humidity and thermal conditions permit. Evaporative assist is conditional and must pass the independent water-rights/quality/reserve governor. No misting or aerosol path is a default.

## 2. Why this is a new benchmark rather than another pool comparison

The existing 10-archetype × 8-scenario pool matrix remains unchanged. This separate 36-case matrix asks whether water-coupled thermal infrastructure can support a refuge as a **time-qualified occupied service** over multiple days, with measured indoor/outdoor conditions, water use, electricity and store regeneration.

It also adds a careful field-transfer comparison to the 2026 Cartuja Qanat Agora study in Seville. That study evaluated a real outdoor climate-adaptation prototype combining near-total solar blocking, a water-fed radiant ceiling, ground/water heat exchange and night radiative–evaporative regeneration. It reports representative extreme-heat occupied-zone air-temperature reductions exceeding 15 °C from outdoor conditions approaching 40 °C, night regeneration capacity of 152 kW, reported COP 32.4, and 80% neutral thermal sensation among surveyed users. Those are **one project's published field results**, not a standard product rating, not a matched comparator, and not a prediction for Johannesburg. The Qanat geometry, scale, climate, plant, operational boundary, occupant load and measurement methods must be reconciled before numerical comparison.

## 3. Six matched system arms

| Arm | System boundary | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| DRY_SHADE_VENTILATION_CONTROL | Competent deep shade, reflective/insulated envelope and safe natural ventilation; no added water | Primary passive control |
| MECHANICAL_REFUGE_REFERENCE | Properly sized mechanical cooling, backup supply as declared, measured plant electricity | Industry service reference |
| RADIANT_WATER_LOOP_NO_STORE | Isolated water-fed radiant surface/ceiling, metered loop, no material storage credit | Isolate radiant transfer |
| CLOSED_LOOP_STORE_GROUND_REGENERATION | Isolated radiant loop plus finite water/PCM buffer and ground exchanger | Test ground-based regeneration |
| CLOSED_LOOP_STORE_RADIATIVE_REGENERATION | Isolated radiant loop plus finite buffer and radiative sky sink | Test sky-based regeneration |
| LAYERED_WATER_BACKED_REFUGE | Shade + radiant loop + finite store + qualified ground/radiative regeneration + optional governed evaporative assist | Integrated portfolio |

The mechanical and dry passive controls must be competent. No candidate may receive more floor area, shaded area, backup power, thermal capacity or capital budget without a separate matched sensitivity arm. Water volume and store capacity are disclosed, not used to hide poor charge/discharge power.

## 4. Six operating scenarios

1. HIGH_VPD_CLEAR_HOT_DAY — high solar load and strong atmospheric moisture demand; record VPD and wet-bulb depression.
2. HUMID_CLOUDY_WEAK_SKY_SINK — high humidity/cloud and weak radiative regeneration.
3. MULTIDAY_HEAT_WAVE_HOT_NIGHTS — limited night recovery and cumulative store headroom stress.
4. GRID_OUTAGE_24H — pump/fan/control/electrical outages and manual safe-state capability.
5. WATER_CURTAILMENT_AND_PROTECTED_RESERVE — no discretionary make-up beyond admitted rights, quality and reserve.
6. COLD_CLEAR_NIGHT_OVERCOOLING — prevent cold-side overshoot and test controlled store regeneration.

Registered matrix: 6 system arms × 6 scenarios = 36 cases. No site weather, event probability, outage duration distribution or result is invented.

## 5. Multi-scale measurement boundary

### Occupied refuge
- operative temperature and mean radiant temperature by occupied zone;
- air temperature, RH/dew point, air speed and vertical temperature gradient;
- occupancy density as anonymous aggregate count and occupied service-hours;
- ventilation/indoor-air-quality status, control state and any air-quality veto;
- comfort evaluation using the selected current method where applicable;
- a separate authority-provided heat-health/emergency service envelope where required.

ASHRAE comfort compliance is not a public-health safety limit. It is also not sufficient to establish service for every population. Any emergency heat-safety envelope must be supplied by a competent local authority/qualified professional with edition, source and applicability metadata.

### Thermal-water loop and store
- loop flow, supply/return temperature, pressure drop and heat exchanger approach;
- radiant surface temperature and measured building-side heat removed;
- water/PCM store state including top/mid/bottom stratification or qualified enthalpy state;
- heat received from the occupied load and heat rejected by each regeneration path;
- pump/fan/valve/treatment electrical energy and parasitic losses;
- night-store state recovery, cycle drift, usable headroom and degradation;
- temperature-limit excursions; report zero positive headroom when over limit, while retaining a failed measurement record.

### Outdoor refuge microclimate
- dry-bulb temperature, RH/dew point, wind direction/speed and exposure;
- globe/mean radiant temperature or a qualified MRT method;
- wet-bulb depression, VPD, downwelling long-wave/cloud boundary and solar irradiance;
- stations upwind, feature edge, near-field, downwind, occupied zone and matched dry control;
- measurement heights and shade/sky-view geometry, with time-blocked rather than falsely independent minute samples.

### Water and environmental boundary
Record make-up, evaporation, splash/drift, leakage, purge/backwash, cleaning, rain and closing volume separately. Identify source class, licence/rights, quality, treatment, biological control and protected reserve. A pool's evaporation is not a zero-cost cooling input. Aerosol-generating systems require a competent water-management programme and independent safety review.

## 6. Core metrics: service first, then efficiency

Report by scenario and interval:
- qualified refuge person-hours within the selected comfort/service envelope;
- occupied-zone discomfort degree-hours and maximum continuous exceedance;
- mean/median/worst-decile operative and radiant conditions;
- heat removed from occupied space (kWh) and peak useful cooling (kW);
- useful service per occupied person-hour and per shaded/occupied m²;
- thermal-store usable kWh, peak charge/discharge kW, headroom, night regeneration and cycle drift;
- energy by pumps/fans/controls/backup (kWh) and peak demand;
- water make-up (L/person-hour and L/useful-kWh), evaporation, drift, leaks, purge and cleaning by source;
- indoor air-quality vetoes, outage service gaps, overcooling and maintenance burden;
- costs, lifecycle replacement, water rights and operational staffing.

A high CoP at the wrong boundary is not a service win. A large air-temperature reduction with poor MRT, humidity, service hours or water burden is not a service win. A water-fed radiant system that removes heat from the room but cannot regenerate its store through hot nights is not a multi-day refuge solution.

## 7. Fair SOTA comparison and transferable evidence

The 2026 Qanat Agora field study is an *integrated-system reference*. Before comparison, reproduce its metric definitions and normalise by the relevant boundary: occupied-zone area/volume, outdoor conditions, occupancy/service-hours, water and electrical boundary, thermal store state, regeneration capacity and instrumentation. Do not compare its reported 152 kW regeneration capacity or >15 °C air-temperature reduction directly with a small house pool, a single material coupon, or one sensor in an unrelated courtyard.

The benchmark baseline set must include:
- competent dry-shade passive control;
- mechanical-cooling refuge reference;
- water-fed radiant loop without storage credit;
- ground regeneration and radiative regeneration separately;
- integrated layered system;
- water-curtailment and over-limit negative controls.

The meaningful improvement is a **Pareto result**: more occupied person-hours in service, lower worst-case service gap, lower water per service-hour, acceptable auxiliary electricity, and store cycle closure under the same weather/outage envelope. No weighted score may hide essential-water breaches, over-temperature or unavailable refuge time.

## 8. Proposed benchmark targets

- 0 protected essential-water reserve breaches.
- 0 unqualified water-source, water-quality or aerosol operation.
- 0 occupied-zone cooling claims without a measured/qualified building-side heat path.
- 0 pool/store capacity counted as a sink without measured regeneration.
- 0 energy or water balance residual beyond propagated uncertainty.
- 0 owner/authority temperature-limit breaches reported as positive headroom.
- 100% input, calibration, property-method, controller, water-rights, weather and trajectory digest traceability.
- 100% seeded negative-control detection with positive controls retained.
- Cycle/seasonal regeneration ratio and worst-case service debt reported; do not set a universal threshold until the refuge owner, load, climate and required service duration are frozen.
- Held-out heat-wave/hot-night and weather regime results, with uncertainty coverage and out-of-domain detection reported separately.

These are proposed targets, not achieved results.
