## 1. Thesis: design for continuity, not immortality

No city, canal, port, lock, railway or seawall can be credibly promised to remain physically unchanged for millions of years. Plate motion, uplift and subsidence, erosion, sedimentation, hydrology, climate, material fatigue and social change will alter the setting.

The durable design target is therefore **continuity of essential services and the ability to renew the infrastructure that provides them**. Model a city as a reconfigurable service network—housing, freshwater, food, energy, health, education, mobility, communications, work and ecological support—not as a fixed polygon or collection of permanent structures.

A corridor may persist as a strategic alignment or recurring function while terminals, channel sections, bridges, locks, rail links and settlements move or are rebuilt. Measure separately:
- service continuity and access;
- asset life, repairability and replacement cost;
- corridor continuity and reroutability;
- ecological and cultural continuity;
- option value and stranded-capital exposure.

This is a research and planning framework, not a proposal to construct a named canal or relocate a community.

## 2. Three different meanings of +200 m

All scenario records must identify the physical mechanism and evidence family. A single slider labelled “sea-level rise” is not sufficient.

### 2.1 Melting today's ice

Complete melting of present-day **land ice** is commonly summarized as about 70 m of global mean sea-level equivalent. Greenland contributes about 7.4 m and Antarctica about 57.9 m, with mountain glaciers and ice caps adding further contribution. Floating sea ice and floating ice shelves are not equivalent to grounded land ice for direct sea-level contribution.

References:
- USGS, *How would sea level change if all glaciers melted?* https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/how-would-sea-level-change-if-all-glaciers-melted
- Copernicus Climate Change Service, ice-sheet indicators: https://climate.copernicus.eu/climate-indicators/ice-sheets
- NASA, sea ice vs land ice: https://sealevel.nasa.gov/faq/23/whats-the-difference-between-melting-sea-ice-and-melting-land-ice-on-sea-levels/

### 2.2 Deep-time geological highstands

A +200 m-class sea-level state is a legitimate **deep-time paleogeographic scenario family**, but not the water equivalent of melting today's ice. Published reconstructions disagree:
- a 2022 paleogeographic-flooding reconstruction estimates a Phanerozoic peak near +280 m around 80 Ma;
- a 2022 tectono-glacio-eustatic reconstruction estimates Late Cretaceous highstands around +174–217 m;
- a 2026 review advances a substantially different Cenozoic reconstruction, placing the Late Cretaceous peak around +40–55 m above present.

These are competing scientific reconstructions with different methods and assumptions. Preserve them as separate source-pinned model families; do not average away disagreement or choose the curve that produces the preferred map.

References:
- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gr.2022.05.011
- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gr.2022.07.014
- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.earscirev.2026.105470

### 2.3 Synthetic +200 m stress test

A +200 m water-surface offset over a modern elevation model is useful as a deliberately extreme **synthetic stress test**. Label it as such. A modern “bathtub” inundation is not a reconstruction of a Cretaceous Earth: it does not represent past continents, plate positions, ocean-basin capacity, dynamic topography, vertical land motion, sedimentation, erosion, isostatic adjustment, drainage or climate.

IPCC AR6 describes low-likelihood/high-impact sea-level outcomes reaching around 2 m by 2100 and exceeding 15 m by 2300 under very high emissions as low-confidence possibilities, not a +200 m anthropogenic forecast. https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/summary-for-policymakers/

**Invariant:** every visualization, export and recommendation must retain scenario family, mechanism, time, reference datum, source curve, uncertainty, spatial resolution and claim ceiling.

## 3. Time horizons and model fitness

| Horizon | Appropriate questions | Claim ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| 0–30 years | Operations, maintenance, hazard response, service continuity | Asset and operational planning, subject to local evidence |
| 30–100 years | Renewal, adaptation, finance, replacement, relocation options | Lifecycle and adaptation pathways |
| 100–500 years | City-region networks, migration, coast and watershed change | Strategic settlement/network scenarios |
| 500–10,000 years | River capture, delta/estuary change, erosion, deposition, groundwater and sediment | Landscape-process ensembles; no precise asset forecasts |
| 10,000–10 million years | Tectonic uplift/subsidence, basin evolution, changing plate boundaries | Probabilistic regional Earth-history scenarios |
| 100–500+ million years | Supercontinent cycles, insolation, atmospheric and planetary boundary conditions | Comparative habitability and paleogeography, not city-scale predictions |

The next-supercontinent literature explores possible configurations roughly 200–250 million years in the future and shows that continental arrangement and changing solar output can radically alter climate. It does not provide a reliable map of future cities. https://doi.org/10.1029/2021GC009983

For Phanerozoic reconstructions, preserve plate-model identity and reference frame. A 2024 gridded palaeogeography dataset compares five global plate models across 540–0 Ma and makes their differences available for analysis: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11217452/

**Precision must decrease with horizon.** A 250-million-year scenario can support comparisons of boundary conditions; it cannot justify asserting that a particular present-day canal alignment will exist or remain navigable.

## 4. Inland waterways are multiple systems, not one “canal” type

Keep the following services distinct:
1. navigation canals and lock canals;
2. tidal/estuarine navigation channels;
3. navigable rivers and lakes;
4. freshwater conveyance and inter-basin transfers;
5. flood bypasses and controlled diversions;
6. ecological corridors for fish, sediment and wetland connectivity;
7. multipurpose corridors, only where each service has its own water, rights, ecology and operating ledger.

### 4.1 The water balance is a hard constraint

For each navigation pool or transfer segment, account for natural inflow, storage releases, precipitation, lockage, pumping, leakage, seepage, evaporation, withdrawals, return flows and legally/ ecologically required downstream flows. For transfers, also quantify donor-basin depletion, conveyance losses, receiving-basin capacity, water quality and invasive-species transfer risks.

A canal does not create water. Navigation demand cannot become an assumed entitlement over municipal, agricultural, ecological or downstream needs.

USACE navigation-waterway guidance treats hydrologic supply, lockage, pumpage/diversion, return flow, seepage/leakage and evaporation as coupled pool-water accounting terms. PIANC's climate guidance addresses ports and inland waterways as systems exposed to changing water levels, rainfall, waves, storms, hydrology and sediment regimes.
- USACE EM 1110-2-1604: https://www.publications.usace.army.mil/Portals/76/Publications/EngineerManuals/EM_1110-2-1604.pdf
- PIANC climate change: https://www.pianc.org/climate-change/
- PIANC waterborne transport climate drivers: https://www.pianc.org/publication/waterborne-transport-ports-and-waterways-a-2023-update-of-climate-change-drivers-and-impacts/

### 4.2 Canal candidate comparison

Compare, under equal demand and service constraints:
- natural river/lake navigation and restoration;
- low-gradient open canal;
- lock canal, ship lift or pumped reach;
- tunnel, aqueduct or pipeline;
- rail-first, dry-port and road/rail intermodal alternatives;
- port upgrade, distributed ports and no-build/demand management.

Model slope and cut/fill, geology, faults, landslides, liquefaction, subsidence/uplift, water availability, environmental flows, evaporation, seepage, salinity, sedimentation, scour, dredging, lockage energy, maintenance, ecological fragmentation, fisheries, cultural landscapes, rights, consent and transboundary obligations.

Design hypotheses worth testing include staged reaches, replaceable lock/gate modules, protected bypasses, parallel rail/road access, habitat and sediment passages, bank/floodplain restoration, and the ability to abandon or repurpose a reach without collapsing the wider logistics network. None is universally beneficial; each must be tested in its local context.

## 5. The paradigm shift: adaptive civilisational infrastructure

The proposed advance is to optimize **portfolios of adaptation pathways**, not one fixed megaproject against one forecast.

1. **From asset permanence to service permanence.** Preserve access to water, food, energy, housing, work and mobility even when individual assets are retired or moved.
2. **From coastline ranking to coast–hinterland systems.** A port's value depends on the freight, people, energy, water, rail, road, border and industrial network behind it.
3. **From single forecast to scenario ensembles.** Compare multiple sea-level reconstructions, local relative sea-level effects, hydrological futures, sediment regimes, demand futures and tectonic boundary conditions.
4. **From maximum expected return to robust regret and option value.** Prefer staged, reversible investments where forecast uncertainty is deep and irreversible costs are large.
5. **From engineered-only to nature-positive alternatives.** Evaluate floodplains, wetlands, mangroves, estuary migration, river restoration and sediment connectivity alongside barriers and channels.
6. **From “more redundancy” to independent failure domains.** Three ports sharing one rail bottleneck, power source or floodplain are not three independent alternatives.
7. **From map output to evidence-carrying decisions.** Every candidate must expose source, age, coverage, datum, uncertainty, transformations, rights, model context, hard-gate disposition and independent reviewer.

Dynamic Adaptive Policy Pathways and PIANC climate adaptation planning provide relevant established baselines. A decision-support system should show thresholds, adaptation triggers, lead times and pathway switch points—not merely a preferred future map.

| Industry/reference capability | What it is good for | Our compositional opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| NOAA Sea Level Rise Viewer; Climate Central screening | Communicating coastal exposure under selected scenarios | Bind exposure to infrastructure dependencies, uncertainty, adaptation options and evidence lineage |
| GEBCO, Copernicus Marine, national hydrography, LiDAR/DEMs | Terrain, bathymetry, waves and environmental context at declared scales | Preserve source fitness, datum, coverage and uncertainty; never treat global grids as design surveys |

| USACE waterway/lock engineering guidance | Established navigation-channel and lock design practice | Couple water availability, lifecycle service, climate scenarios and alternatives at the corridor level |
| GPlates, PALEOMAP and published plate/palaeogeography models | Reconstructing past plate positions and palaeo-environments | Keep competing reconstructions and time-dependent terrain provenance visible |
| DAPP / robust decision-making | Adaptation under uncertainty and trigger-based planning | Extend the workflow to ports, canals, inland networks, cities and service continuity |

Do not benchmark a Luminous research adapter against an entire commercial suite by feature count. Compare a named task, same inputs, geography, resolution, boundary conditions, model maturity, compute budget, validation data and output metric.

**We should not try to replace mature coastal hydrodynamics or global climate science.** Our strongest differentiator is an evidence-qualified layer that coordinates specialist models, alternatives, network consequences, uncertainty, rights and adaptive decision pathways.

## 7. Benchmark programme

Build on the existing 30-case synthetic Port-City Foundry corpus (#378). Add at least 40 frozen deep-time, climate, canal and adaptation cases, then hold out public reference cases and geographic regions.

### A. Scenario integrity
- Correctly distinguish land-ice equivalent, IPCC climate projections, geological eustatic reconstructions, local relative sea level and synthetic stress tests.
- Zero mislabeled scenario mechanisms or untraceable source curves.
- Preserve disagreement between paleosea-level reconstructions.
- Report inundation sensitivity to DEM/bathymetry resolution, vertical datum and elevation uncertainty.
- Keep historical information cut-offs so future information cannot leak into past decision replays.

### B. Hydrology and channel accounting
- Exact agreement with hand-calculable water-ledger fixtures.
- Synthetic water-balance residual target: <=0.1% for deterministic accounting cases.
- Negative controls for drought, evaporation, seepage, leakage, lockage, sedimentation, salinity, ecological-flow deficits and water-rights conflicts.
- No “feasible” disposition where supply, environmental flows, donor-basin effects, rights or receiving-basin constraints are unknown.
- Compare specialist hydraulic models against analytical, laboratory and field references before using them for real-site decisions.

### C. Network resilience and infrastructure outcomes
- Compare canal/port/rail/road portfolios against rail-first, dry-port, natural-waterway, existing-port upgrade and no-build options under the same demand and constraints.
- Measure freight/passenger throughput, travel time, delivered service, unmet demand, lifecycle cost, maintenance, water/energy use, ecological disturbance, failure cut sets, independent route availability, recovery time and stranded capital.
- Report Pareto fronts, sensitivity, regret, rank reversals and uncertainty—not only one weighted score.
- Compare staged/adaptive pathways with one-shot megaprojects under equal budgets.

### D. Evidence, reproducibility and safety
- 100% correct blocked / ineligible / NeedsEvidence disposition on planted negative controls; zero false-safe or false-eligible results.
- 100% deterministic replay of pinned synthetic scenario, water-ledger and portfolio fixtures.
- Independent verifier that does not call production validation/scoring code.
- Exact result on hand-checkable fixtures; public reference tolerance declared before running.
- Report performance, memory and solver cost only for matched workloads and hardware.

These are proposed promotion targets, not measured results.

## 8. Luminous system responsibilities

- **Sol-Atlas:** geographic and temporal evidence; source/CRS/vertical-datum lineage; spatial discovery; scenario and portfolio presentation; uncertainty and claim-ceiling visibility.
- **Symtropy:** deterministic scenario orchestration, network and lifecycle simulation, water-ledger accounting, and versioned adapters to validated external specialist solvers. It must not claim its rigid-body engine simulates fluid dynamics.
- **Symthaea:** generate competing design hypotheses, reason across scenario ensembles, rank investigation actions, explain trade-offs and propose adaptive pathways. It cannot create missing evidence or self-certify.
- **Mycelix:** preserve participant identity, source rights, access/consent, provenance, commitments, reviewer actions and revocations. A trust receipt is not a permit, property title, water allocation or engineering approval.

Use the existing owner-native components and issue tree; do not create a second GIS, asset registry, Pareto engine, EVSI kernel, physical solver or authority store.

## 9. First implementation sequence

1. Define a typed time-horizon and scenario-family manifest that composes the existing port-alternative contract (#372).
2. Freeze source-pinned scenario records for present land-ice equivalent, IPCC 2100/2300 cases, competing Cretaceous reconstructions and synthetic +200 m.
3. Add model-family and paleogeography lineage, preserving plate model, reference frame, time slice and transformation history.
4. Add hand-computable navigation-pool and inter-basin-transfer water ledgers with independent expected outputs.
5. Add explicit water rights, environmental-flow, ecology, source-quality and physical-model applicability gates.
6. Extend the synthetic case corpus to 40+ cases and build an independent verifier.
7. Only then select one regional reference reconstruction after data rights, local topography/bathymetry, water balance, ecology, geology and community/authority context have been audited.
8. Integrate a read-only adaptation-pathway view in Sol-Atlas, with exact model and source receipts.

**First data rule:** classify every infrastructure and maglev record as observed, proposed, derived, curated or synthetic. A visually plausible route or cost estimate is not an operating asset, a feasibility study or an approved project.

## 10. Claim ceiling

A passing synthetic benchmark proves only the tested contract and calculation behavior. It does not establish climate forecast skill, paleogeographic truth, navigability, local water availability, geotechnical safety, ecological acceptability, community consent, legal authority, financing or engineering feasibility. Site-specific work requires qualified professionals, lawful rights-holders, affected communities and competent authorities.
